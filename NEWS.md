## LEAD 1 — BELGIUM · EUROPE
**A national strike brings Belgium to a standstill**
Belgium's unions shut the country down on Monday in protest at Prime
Minister Bart De Wever's austerity and labour reforms. Air traffic
control stopped from 7am to 9pm, most of the 429 flights due at
Brussels Airport were cancelled — around 59,500 passengers hit — and
Charleroi airport closed entirely. Trains ran a reduced service, only
about six in ten buses and trams ran in Flanders, and a national
demonstration marched through Brussels from the Gare du Nord at 11am
to the Gare du Midi.
- EU Today (eutoday.net): air traffic control suspended 7am–9pm; most
  of 429 Brussels Airport flights cancelled; about 59,500 passengers
  affected; national demonstration in Brussels.
- Tragento (tragento.com): Brussels Airport flights suspended 7am–9pm;
  Charleroi closed to takeoffs and landings; march from Bruxelles-Nord
  at 11am to Bruxelles-Midi; De Lijn running about 60% of buses and
  trams.

## MEDIUM 8 — GERMANY · EUROPE
**IG Metall calls 23,000 workers out on Monday**
Germany's IG Metall union said on Sunday it would call another
24-hour strike on Monday, sending 23,000 workers at 19 companies
across northern Germany — among them Mercedes-Benz, Meyer Werft and
Siemens Gamesa — out in a pay dispute that keeps escalating. The union
wants a 5% wage rise; employers have offered no permanent rise for the
first 20 months and 1.3% from July 2028. On Friday, about 12,300
workers struck at 17 sites.
- Reuters (reuters.com, 22 Nov): IG Metall said on Sunday it would
  launch another 24-hour strike on Monday, calling on 23,000 workers
  across 19 companies in northern Germany to walk out; Mercedes-Benz,
  Meyer Werft, Siemens Gamesa; demands 5%.
- DW (dw.com): IG Metall union calls for new 24-hour strike.

**New details link Alice Huang to a chip-smuggling web**
A Financial Times investigation tied Alice Huang, the businesswoman
linked to former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, to a network
that moved AI chips to China-based users, including an alleged
Cambodian criminal enterprise. The Wall Street Journal reported that
Li Ming, a central operator of the smuggling web, is out on bail in
Singapore. Nvidia says all its shipments were legal and authorised,
and that it put Megaspeed on its block list in June 2025.
- Financial Times (via TradingView / Invezz): FT finds Alice Huang
